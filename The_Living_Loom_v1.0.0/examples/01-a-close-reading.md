# A map that survives an experience

## The source situation

A Dana Sorman Instagram post dated May 16, with the year inferred as 2026 in the private chronology, describes a state of intense connection followed by a return to ordinary time. The source is available to this release only through a reviewed private screenshot pair and exact record. The public locator and the original audio or additional media are unavailable.

No source wording is reproduced here because redistribution permission is unresolved.

## First reading

Based on the limited-access screenshot record, the Loom provisionally reads a contrast between intense connection and later longing. That is present, but it is not yet the most useful structure. This is interpretation, not a quotation or a claim about Dana's intended instruction.

The Loom's source-local reconstruction is more precise:

1. an integrated high-connection state;
2. its recession;
3. sequentialized relational markers;
4. a shift from immediate knowing to desire;
5. a mapping role for what remains.

The Loom therefore interprets the later state as carrying a task: retain a portable structure after direct access is gone.

## Strongest insight

Continuity does not require permanent access to the originating state. It requires preserving the relations that remain action-relevant after the state changes.

That is stronger than “remember how you felt.” A feeling is one payload. A map preserves distinctions among nodes, routes, milestones, validation signals, and openings. The passage therefore separates experience from the structure exported from experience.

## Rival

An ordinary psychological reading explains the same movement as memory consolidation after an intense experience. The language of maps and primers may be metaphorical scaffolding rather than evidence of a literal communication network.

The rival does not destroy the Loom reading. It limits its ontological reach. Both support the design principle that post-experience structure matters; they differ on what kind of network is being described.

## Boundary

Certainty inside an altered or ceremonial state does not verify every proposition felt there. A map can also be selective, distorted, or overfit. The next test is recoverability: can another person use the preserved relations without being told to reproduce the original state?

## New construction

The public Loom applies this distinction to itself. The private archive is not portable as a whole. The starter therefore preserves a method, source boundaries, worked relations, and routes back to evidence. This repository design is a Living Loom construction, not an instruction attributed to Dana.
