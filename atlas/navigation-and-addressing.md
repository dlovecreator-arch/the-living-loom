# Navigation and addressing

A destination, its address, the route toward it, and the event of arriving are different objects.

The Loom repeatedly encounters representations used as maps, coordinates, primers, targets, or interfaces. The gain comes from separating their functions. An image may help select a destination without containing the route. A keyword may validate a connection without granting access. A map may orient without reproducing the territory.

Boundary: a compelling representation does not prove that it causally generates the event it depicts. It may coordinate attention, behavior, memory, or group action instead.

Open question: when does a representation merely describe a possible world, and when does it change the probability of coordinated movement toward it?
